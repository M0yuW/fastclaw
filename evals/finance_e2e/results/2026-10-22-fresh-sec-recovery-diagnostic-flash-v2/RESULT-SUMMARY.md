# Same-case repair diagnostic v2: result

Six observations (two repeats each for AAPL, MSFT and SBUX) completed on fresh sessions after a general causal-claim error-message/tool-description repair and a general adjacent-quarter comparison instruction. All six had a linked guarded review, alert acknowledgement and correct ledger versioning. Estimated model spend was **$0.012016** under the $0.04 diagnostic cap. Raw observations and manifest are frozen in `RESULT-SHA256.txt`.

Codex's first-pass, non-blinded F/I/R/S/U review found **3 high within case, 3 adequate, 0 problematic**; individual reasons are in `INSIGHT-JUDGE-v1.csv`. The previously recurring refusal to compare adjacent-quarter levels did not recur: the model computed appropriate level differences while keeping disclosed year-over-year rates distinct. Both SBUX repeats completed without the v1 tool-iteration exhaustion. Every run first proposed a historical `next_review_at` and recovered after the tool rejected it; this remains an efficiency/recovery issue, not a successful first-try tool input. AAPL's two answers left a legitimate qualitative, source-explicit tax-to-EPS link out of structured `causal_claims` because they incorrectly inferred that a driver must be quantified before it can be recorded. That limits causal provenance despite accurate narrative separation of GAAP and adjusted EPS.

This was a **post-hoc, same-case repair diagnostic**, not a new issuer holdout. Its 6/6 mechanical and 3/6 high labels cannot be pooled with or substituted for the original forward panel's 14/15 mechanical and 0/15 high results. The v1 failure remains a negative finding. The next test of generalization requires new companies/cases and multiple finance workflows, a frozen design before outputs, independent source checks, and repeat runs. Neither experiment establishes predictive investment value or a population-level reliability rate; the judge remains Codex rather than an independent financial reviewer.
