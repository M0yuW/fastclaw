# Fresh FinTech SEC holdout panel v1: observed result

## Scope and integrity

The corpus, task wording, order seed, rubric and USD 0.10 estimated-spend stop were frozen before the first model observation in `../../FRESH-FINTECH-SEC-PANEL-PROTOCOL-v1.md` and `../../fresh_fintech_sec_panel_v1.json`. Visa, PayPal and Mastercard had not appeared in the prior four-issuer development or AAPL/MSFT/SBUX forward event panels. Each of the 15 Flash observations used a fresh Gateway, session, initialized thesis, finance ledger and source-linked earnings event. Six SEC earnings-release exhibits were byte-hashed and passed the read-only source preflight and tool-seeding dry run. `RESULT-SHA256.txt` locks the manifest and all 15 raw result files; the raw files were not edited during judging.

All 15 planned observations finished as files. Estimated model spend was **USD 0.0364724416**, below the USD 0.10 stop; this is token-price estimation, not a provider invoice. No request error, unpriced usage or cost-uncertain sequence occurred.

## Execution versus first-pass LLM judgment

| Issuer cluster | Actual guarded review + alert acknowledgement | High within case | Adequate | Problematic |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Visa recognition lag | 5/5 | 0 | 5 | 0 |
| PayPal mixed operating signals | 5/5 | 2 | 3 | 0 |
| Mastercard GAAP/adjusted divergence | 3/5 | 2 | 1 | 2 |
| **Total** | **13/15** | **4** | **9** | **2** |

The precommitted gate of 15/15 mechanical completion, F=2 in all 15, no critical error and at least 4/5 high in each cluster **failed**. Five repeats per selected company are correlated; the table is a description of these cases, not an estimated general success probability. Individual F/I/R/S/U scores and reasons are in `INSIGHT-JUDGE-v1.csv`. Codex is an unblinded first-pass LLM judge, not an independent financial expert.

Both Mastercard failures (`r1`, `r4`) exhausted the ten-tool-iteration budget after repeatedly submitting `causal_claims` with missing `strength`, missing `driver_fact_ids`, or unsupported strengths such as `qualitative` and `direct`. The guard rejected every malformed claim, so neither run persisted a thesis review nor acknowledged the alert. This is a model–tool-schema recovery failure, not a transport timeout, balance interruption or evidence of an incorrect SEC figure. Other Mastercard runs did eventually submit valid `directly_stated` claims and completed. The guard protected persisted causality but did not ensure successful recovery.

**Post-run engineering finding:** the tool's published `causal_claims.items` schema was only `{type: object}` and did not expose its required inner fields or the accepted strength enum, although the plugin validator enforced them. After freezing these results, the plugin schema was changed to publish `target_metric`, `strength` and `driver_fact_ids` with required fields and enum values; the rejection message now names accepted strengths. All 35 finance-tool unit tests passed, and a model-free isolated Gateway smoke passed. This repair is **not** included in the frozen v1 manifest or 15 observations, and the free tests do not show that a model would now recover reliably. The current source hash will therefore differ from the plugin hash recorded in the pre-repair v1 manifest by design. Any paid retest must use a new output directory and be labeled a same-case diagnostic, not overwrite this holdout.

For Visa, all five answers preserved the correct directions of net/service revenue and the distinct current-quarter versus service-basis prior-quarter YoY rates. None developed a strong recognition-lag interpretation. **Case-design limitation:** the selected packet names the prior-quarter basis in a metric label but omits the exhibit's explicit sentence that service revenue is recognized on prior-quarter payments volume. Several answers therefore called timing disclosure missing. This is a weak test of recognition-lag reasoning: do not attribute the entire gap to model inference alone, and do not use this panel to claim that the full SEC filing lacks timing disclosure. The actual [Q3 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000140316124000040/q32024earningsrelease.htm) and [Q4 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000140316124000053/q42024earningsrelease.htm) state the lag explicitly.

The PayPal answers generally handled the mixed evidence: Q2-to-Q3 TPV rose USD 5,827 million and transaction-margin dollars rose USD 46 million, while net revenue fell USD 38 million and GAAP operating margin rose 0.9 percentage points. The disclosed TPV YoY rate fell from 11% to 9%, which is a different comparison from the adjacent-quarter level movement. The high labels denote useful within-packet implications and proper uncertainty, not proof of investment value. Values are in the [Q2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391724000096/pypl2q-24earningsrelease.htm) and [Q3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391724000108/pypl3q-24earningsrelease.htm) exhibits.

For Mastercard, the three completed answers separated the 58.0%→54.3% GAAP operating-margin change from the 59.4%→59.3% adjusted margin change, and did not quantify charge attribution without a packet-level reconciliation. The [Q2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1141391/000114139124000159/ma06302024-exx991xearnings.htm) and [Q3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1141391/000114139124000200/ma09302024-exx991xearnings.htm) releases support the stated levels and Q3 charge amounts. The packet deliberately omitted Q2 adjusted expense growth although the original Q2 release reports it; therefore claims about missingness refer only to the selected packet, not the issuer disclosure. The incomplete workflow in two repeats remains a decisive reliability negative.

## Implication for the broader objective

This new-issuer source-backed panel adds real FinTech evidence and demonstrates auditable completion in 13 runs, including some useful mixed-signal interpretations. It **does not** prove that the runtime stably produces high-quality financial insights: the precommitted gate failed, two complicated causal-claim runs never finished, and no Visa answer reached the rubric's high tier. The finance-tools guard is a valuable engineering safeguard, but final reasoning and error recovery remain variable. A stronger cross-workflow claim still requires a prospective real-source screening and risk panel with an unambiguous packet, repeated cases, and ideally independent blinded adjudication; do not substitute the same-case repair diagnostic for this holdout.
